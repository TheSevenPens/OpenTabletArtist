## What's new

**The pen page explains its own choices.** On the Pen page, Movement and Pressing are
dropdowns now, and each choice carries a sentence about what it does when you open the
list. Closed, the box shows just the name. Position smoothing moved up under Movement, with
the thing it actually steadies, instead of sitting last under Pressing.

**The pressure page shows what the curve does to a stroke.** Two canvases side by side take
the same pen: one draws the pressure as your tablet reports it, the other the pressure after
your curve and smoothing. The readouts under the smoothing slider show the raw and processed
values and the pressure deadzone, the point on the curve where the pen starts to register.
Pressure that the curve maps to nothing now leaves no ink in the processed canvas, which is
the same rule the Pen Dynamics filter applies to what your drawing app receives.

**Scribble draws what your apps will receive.** With Pen Dynamics on, the Scribble canvas
now draws the processed pressure, like the pressure page's processed canvas, so a curve you
have set can be seen on a stroke. The readouts above the canvas still show the pressure the
tablet reported. With Pen Dynamics off, nothing changes: you get the raw pressure, which is
what your apps get.

**Scribble can record strokes for the Stroke Corpus.** Record keeps every report while you
draw. Stop, and a save window shows where every report went, which tablet it was, and asks
for the details that make a recording useful: a name for it, the firmware, which pen, who
you are, and any notes. The firmware and the pen are remembered for each tablet, with a note when a value was
carried over, because a different pen is easy to forget to change. Everything on the form is
saved in the recording, and the window says so: if you contribute a recording to the Stroke
Corpus, it is published with all of it, under CC BY 4.0.

**Save tells you when there is something to save.** When you have unsaved changes, the Save
button reads SAVE with an alert icon after it, and its tooltip says so too.

**Home no longer reports VMulti as missing when it is installed.** OTA's own installer
creates the VMulti device under a name the check did not look for, so on a machine where
pressure and tilt worked, Home showed a Broken "VMulti driver not installed" card. It
recognises the device now. The VMulti install and uninstall also keep their output in the
app log, so when one fails there is something to read.

## Fixed

- **Scribble drew nothing with a monitor to the left of or above the main one,** because the
  pen position was measured from a different corner than the canvas.
- **The Home page's "User manual" link opens the documentation site.**

## Also

Scribble shows how many reports per second your tablet is sending, and its other readouts are
steadier at high report rates and go blank when the pen leaves range. The documentation is
now published as a site at https://thesevenpens.github.io/OpenTabletArtist/.

Work continues on macOS and Linux, but only the Windows build is released and supported.
